## Frequently Asked Questions

Earn

### What do users earn yield on?

Supported stablecoins, such as USDC, supplied to third-party lending markets on Base. Supported assets vary by market.

### How does a business generate revenue?

Depending on your product design, you may keep a share of the yield earned on customer balances you supply to lending markets. You set any yield share, disclose it to your customers, and are responsible for meeting applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

### Where does the yield come from?

Deposits are supplied to third-party lending protocols on Base, such as Aave and Morpho, either directly or through vaults run by independent curators. Borrowers pay interest on loans from those deposits, and that interest is the main source of yield. Loans are overcollateralized when made, but collateral can lose value, and liquidation may not fully repay a loan.

### How much yield can customers earn?

Rates are variable, change often and are not guaranteed. The Markets page shows the current average USD stablecoin lending rate on Base, updated daily. What customers receive also depends on the platform's yield share and fees.

### Why Base?

Base has deep, live lending markets: $3.41B supplied and $2.54B borrowed across 135 markets as of 2026-10-02. Platforms can use existing markets rather than building their own. Live rates and liquidity are on the Markets page.

### What is a curator?

A curator is an independent team that runs a vault. It chooses which lending markets the vault supplies to and sets its risk limits. Base and Coinbase do not curate, manage or guarantee any vault.

### What are the risks?

Lending and borrowing through third-party protocols carries risk, including loss of funds. Key risks include:

Smart contract risk: Protocols may contain bugs or vulnerabilities. Audits, where available, reduce but do not eliminate this risk.

Collateral and liquidation risk: Loans are overcollateralized, and protocols sell collateral if its value falls below a set threshold. In fast markets, or if a price feed fails, liquidation may not fully repay the loan, and lenders may take losses.

Curator risk: Independent curators choose which markets a vault lends to and set its risk limits. Their decisions can cause losses. Caps and allocations are visible onchain but can change at any time.

Price feed and stablecoin risk: Price data can be wrong or delayed, and stablecoins or wrapped assets can lose their peg.

### Can customers lose money?

Yes. Lending through third-party protocols carries risk, including loss of funds from smart contract failures, liquidations that do not fully repay a loan, curator decisions, or a stablecoin losing its peg. See “What are the risks?”

### What if many customers withdraw at once?

Withdrawals draw on supplied funds that are not currently lent out. If most of a market's funds are borrowed, some withdrawals may be delayed until borrowers repay, new deposits arrive, or loans are liquidated. There is no guaranteed timeframe.

When a market is highly used, its borrow and supply rates typically rise, which can encourage repayments and new deposits. This does not guarantee that funds will become available. Vault withdrawals also depend on the markets the vault lends to and on curator allocations. Protocols may pause or restrict withdrawals under their terms, and losses from bad debt may reduce what lenders can withdraw.

Platforms are responsible for explaining withdrawal timing and risks to their customers.

### Who holds the funds?

Base does not hold or custody customer funds. While deposited, funds sit in third-party lending protocol smart contracts. Who controls the wallet and signs transactions depends on the integrating platform and product design. The platform is responsible for explaining this to its customers.

### How long does it take to launch?

Timing depends on the integration, wallet and custody model, market and curator review, security testing, customer disclosures, and applicable legal and regulatory requirements. Using an existing supported market may reduce implementation time but does not guarantee launch or availability. We scope timing with you up front.

Borrow

### What can users borrow against?

Supported onchain assets, which may include bitcoin (cbBTC), ETH and, in eligible jurisdictions, tokenized stocks. Supported collateral and loan assets, such as USDC, vary by market.

### How does a business generate revenue?

You may charge an origination or processing fee or receive other agreed compensation, depending on implementation and applicable requirements. You are responsible for any licensing and for disclosing fees and loan terms to your customers.

### How does borrowing work?

A user supplies collateral to an onchain lending market on Base, such as Morpho, and draws USDC against it. Every loan is overcollateralized: the user posts more value than they borrow. Loan-to-value limits and liquidation terms vary by market.

### What happens if collateral value falls?

The user can repay part of the loan or add collateral. If collateral value falls below the market's liquidation threshold, the protocol automatically sells collateral to repay the loan. Liquidation can happen quickly and without notice, may include a penalty, and may not fully repay the loan. Tokenized stock collateral may be liquidated when traditional markets are closed.

### Who holds the collateral?

Base does not hold or custody collateral. While a loan is open, collateral is locked in the third-party lending protocol's smart contracts. Who controls the wallet and signs transactions depends on the integrating platform and product design. The platform is responsible for explaining this to its customers.

### What does it cost to borrow?

A variable interest rate, any platform fees, and network fees. Liquidations may carry additional penalties. Current borrow rates by collateral are on the Markets page, updated daily.

### How long does it take to launch?

Timing depends on the integration, wallet and custody model, market and curator review, security testing, customer disclosures, and applicable legal and regulatory requirements. Using an existing supported market may reduce implementation time but does not guarantee launch or availability. We scope timing with you up front.
